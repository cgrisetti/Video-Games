# Everwood

A cooperative raid action-RPG for a family party, built in [Godot 4.7](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/) (GDScript, Jolt physics). Up to 4 players share one screen in split-screen, and up to 5 play together over the network. It's a separate project from Lanternwood: no Vite, no three.js, no launcher page.

## What's playable

**The Caldera Isles** ([docs/20](docs/20_CALDERA_ATLAS_AND_CROWN_BIBLE.md)), where every launch begins: five islands in a drowned volcano, one streamed at a time, with the other four on the horizon. You start in the **Lee-Shore Orchards** of **Miller's Oak** (Lv 1-6) by the Windmill, among curious Bristle-Hobs, and walk the lantern-poled roads (laid over the land by the best way, cobbled in small setts or earthen on the footpaths, [docs/26](docs/26_ROADS_LAID_OVER_THE_LAND.md)) to **Hearthstead Market** (the Chime-Alchemist, Blacksmith, Innkeeper and Calling Tutor, and three lane stalls shuttered until their Beacons burn) and **North Quay**, where the World-Tree, The Crown's great tree, stands across The Sound. The **Root-Sanctum**'s door leads into the Cairnsman's expedition; lull him, and the trade lanes go dark and three ways open: the **West Strait** causeway to **Iron-Ness** and the **Eel Spit** shingle to **Barrow-Eyot** (walked through sea-fog), and the **Salt Road** skiff bell to **Rime-Firth**. Each friction isle has a steading, a guarded **Beacon** (calm its kin, then light kindles it: its stall stocks at home, its current round The Crown calms, its Sign is earned), kin camps, its landmark and a sealed raid gate on its wild outer shore. Three Beacons open the **Crown Landing**: **The Crown** (Lv 15-20, [docs/23](docs/23_THE_CROWN_ZONE_REDESIGN.md)) has no town, just the Lantern-Bearers' Waypost (the Quartermaster and the Warden) and four shores round a sheer +32 m cliff band, each with its own ground, landmark, kin and music: **South Bay** (the gully, the stream's fall and the One Cliff Stair), **Iron Shore** (ironstone shelves and Old King's Kiln), the **Gale Cliffs** (the Breach Point and its Sanctum Arch) and the **Tide Grottos** (the Weeping Arch and the Sea-Door). The Ring Path loops through three warm-spring checkpoints, each with a scout, and passes three open-air trials (the Anvil, the Gale, the Tide) on short spurs; each trial blooms a bough of **the World-Tree**. The One Cliff Stair climbs to the +65 m Summit Glade, where the whole party must pull the **Harmonic Bell** together. Then the sea falls six metres on every isle (a ten-second cinematic) and bares the Old Quay Market, the three raid gates and the **Sea-Door** to the Sap Galleries and the Heart. The raids behind the gates and the Heart's True Raid aren't built yet. `-- --boot-zone=crown` starts at The Crown's landing, `north_quay` at North Quay; **F9** warps between Miller's Oak and The Crown (a playtest: nothing changed is saved). No minimap: steer by the World-Tree and the far isles.

**Legacy maps** (in `deprecated/legacy_maps/`, still loadable): **Oakshire Vale** (`-- --instance=oakshire_vale`), the old landlocked starting zone: a streamed world 1.5 km square, 12 x 12 Landblocks. You arrive at Wayfarer-Green and follow the lantern-lit road north over the Bridge-Warden's span to Oakshire-Wick on its saddle, among 68 townsfolk (quest givers under a gold !, smiths who open the Armory, apothecaries who mend you). Five petal valleys ring the village, each with a camp, its own creatures (Curious Weald-Wights, Padfoot Boggarts, Furrow Iron-Men, Bramble-Hobs, Crag-Spriggans) and, in four of them, a portal apron in the rim crags sealed by a Scribe Sign until the party's level opens it. The river is a 0.4 m ford you splash through, and the Crag-Back Cairn-Drake walks the High Road. The dungeons behind the portals and a quest log aren't built yet (12 §6).

**Bramble-Bridge's seamless world** (legacy; start it with `-- --instance=bramblebridge`). The party wakes round a campfire at **the Kindle-Fire**, on the woodland trailhead south of Bramble-Bridge. Stand by the fire to warm up to full, then follow the walled lane east and north through **Honey-Wick** (a stepped-gable homestead, bee-hives and heirloom pumpkin patches), in at Bramble-Bridge's south gate and over the canal bridge. Once anyone reaches the village square, that's where the party gets back up and where a raid brings it home. The whole expedition lies north of the village with no loading screen: walk out of the open Forest Gate, under the Moss-Oak Great Arch, through the Moss-Oaks and down Rush-Light-Hollow, up a trail to Doverslap Bridge and the Doverslap Gatehouse, and up the causeway to the Ring of the Cairnsman. The world is made of 128 m Landblocks that stream in round every player (all four split-screen views and everyone on the network), with far-off ones shown as simple silhouettes. Cleared camps, opened chests, the shrine's rest and the open gate stay that way as you come and go. AI companions join once anyone leaves the village, and after the Cairnsman a gold portal brings everyone home for a fresh run. The Raid Portal in the village still warps the party into the expedition as its own instance. Press **F4** to see the world's streaming in a corner badge.

**The Doverslap Falls Expedition**, the raid, in three zones end to end. **Zone 1**: Moss-Oak Hollow and Rush-Light-Hollow, with two Hush-Cracks of slumbering woodland kin (Bramble-Hobs and Wisp-Boggles, who fly off as hedge-wrens and rise as path-lights when beaten), clay Wind-Lanterns that chime as you pass, the Wayfarer's Water-Shrine (walk through its mist and the whole party is restored), and the Wayfarer's Chest hidden in a side alcove (Chimes and an Uncommon card). **Zone 2**: Doverslap Bridge and the Doverslap Gatehouse, whose portcullis opens for two players standing on both Maker-Seal Plates for 2 seconds (a companion takes the second plate in solo play), or for a teammate ringing the Bronze Bridge-Bell while sheltered from the Gorge Gale-Jet by a Shield-Sworn's Shadow Sanctuary. **Zone 3**: the Ring of the Cairnsman and its boss, **The Cairnsman of Doverslap Falls**, a sentinel of stacked mossy river-boulders with boulder fists, bridge timbers, brass bell-ropes and a glowing amber core. Reaching a zone moves everyone's respawn to its checkpoint.

**The Trail of the Four Signs.** A new party starts at Level 1 with lathe-turned timber (the Turned-Hazel Falchion, Reed-Light Way-Staff or Willow-Coppice Longbow) and the Bramble-Spun Jerkin. Every button works from the start: with no card in R or L+R, the weapon itself swings an **Un-Inked Martial Flourish** (the Falchion's Timber-Cross Slash, say, for 1 pip). Mobs, the Gorge gate and bosses pay XP; each new level flies a Saffron-Gold Wayfarer Pennant over every hero, and milestones drop straight into empty slots: Hedgerow Iron at Level 2, a first Plain Ink R card at 3, Bramble-Bridge Steel and an L+R card at 5, an Under-Card beneath R at 7, and a Doverslap relic and the Secondary Callings at 10. One expedition takes the party to about Level 5, a second to 10. Old saves start at Level 5 or 10 by their Cairnsman wins.

**Move while you fight.** Every swing steps you forward (Back + heavy hops you back), and three Lights in place leave you winded on the third. Weave Forward + heavy, Back + heavy, R, L + R, a guard or a roll into the first moment of a Light's recovery for a **Flow-Link Pulse** (stamina back, a sliver of pip, a white-gold ring). Hit an enemy three times from the front and it raises a **Braced Front Guard**: the front takes 30% damage and a red shield-shove follows, so the tank cracks it with Iron-Root or holds it while someone lands a Forward + heavy, Back + heavy or R on its back for a **Flank Breach**. R and L + R swell your weapon to twice its size in your card's colour; a seal turns a Sign Ring under the enemy with a badge over its head, and your R diamond rings gold when a teammate's seal is yours to set off.

**Sign-Flow: "Coat, Break, Turn!"** Each of the L, R and L+R diamonds holds a diorama: an **Ink Card** (what you do: an Ink Blade, an Ink Shield or a Lantern-Tether) framed by a **Place-Card** (where you stand: Moss-Oak Hollow, Doverslap Bridge, the Bramble-Bridge Hearth-Mill or Barrow-Crag-Crossing). A Place-Card lends its strength (+15 poise, +0.8 m reach, a mending ember ring, stamina back on every Flow-Link), and one of the leaf's own Sign is **Home-Ground Harmony**: a gold star and a 25% longer coat. Out of combat, hold R or L+R for 2 s for the free **Hearth-Rite**: your hero draws a glowing ink card from their coat pocket and holds it overhead as the Sign's ink pours down, then INK READY: a pip in hand, the weapon glowing at 1.35x, a 15% ward against the next foe, and an opening strike that carries the Sign into the next fight. In combat, land a hit, then tap R, L+R or L in the short **Flow-Link Window** (longer near a Lantern-Bearer or on its Soul Tether): the move goes off at once, your off hand sweeps the card along the weapon as it flares to 2x, your camera pulses in, a 2.5 m Sign ring stamps the ground, and you're coated in the Sign for 8 s (10 on Home-Ground). Strike a boss's golden **Glowing Spot** with a Sign that counters it for a **Parry & Strike**: a huge poise hit and the Sign's detonation. The Cairnsman opens his as he roars into each raid cast (Iron-Root or Star-Tide cracks him); his window and HUD rhyme shrink as the family learns him. Minor kin crack under any Iron-Root strike. A Squire who presses just before the roar has the counter fire for them.

**Combos, roles and tougher foes.** Short combo forms finish with a bigger blow, using the buttons you already have: Light, Light, Heavy steps in for 1.5x; Light, Light, Forward + heavy is the **Sundering Gap-Leap** (2.8 m and a stun); Light, Light, Back + heavy vaults you back untouchable; Heavy, then Heavy with the stick sideways circles to the foe's flank; Forward + heavy, R, L + R stretches the Sign's detonation. Pips under your weapon diamond show how far into a form you are. Each role's coat does its own thing: a DPS spends an Ink Blade coat with L + R on a 3.2x **One-and-Done**; a Tank's **Ink Shield** soaks 80% of a blow of its Sign (an enemy's striking limb glows that colour as it winds up), shrugs off the stun and knocks 60 poise back; a Lantern-Bearer's Soul Tether wards an ally against their next stun, and their R or L + R lends its Sign to everyone nearby for 8 s (**Ink-Share**), so plain swings crack that Sign's weak spots. From Level 3, Oakshire's kin fight back harder: at 75% health they leap away, drop a piece of armour and show a glowing lock (white-gold for a Gap-Leap, a Sign's colour for that Sign). Crack it for a Parry & Strike and a long stun; plain hits still work at half damage. The Cairnsman fights in four gates: at 75% he hides behind an Aegis Mantle and calls three Bramble-Hobs; at 50% comes the Gale-Beam and a new weakness; at 25% he roars and shows his upside-down Maker-Seal, and an Iron-Root strike that turns it wins the fight on the spot. At the Scribery, a Gilded Leaf card is 10% stronger and Star-Ink 20%.

**Spirits, not monsters (the Mythopoetic Lens).** Every foe is a spirit of the First-Quiet, the earth's old sleep rising through cracks in the land (docs/15). Oakshire's Bristle-Hobs and Hedgerow-Hobs are curious, not hostile: walk within 6 m and, for 3 seconds, light (J / X / □, the Squire's one button) waves the Truce-Sign, the hob dances, and the party gets 25 Chimes and you a pip. Strike one instead and it turns boggart. Nothing leaves a body: a beaten padfoot flies off as a white sea-swallow, Bramble-Hobs as hedge-wrens, Wisp-Boggles rise as path-lights, Crag-Spriggans and the Cairnsman curl into mossy cairns, and a Furrow Iron-Man whose upside-down chest seal you open with a Warm-Hearth strike ("feed him a coal") and turn with an Iron-Root strike bows as a friendly steward for 10 minutes. The Crag-Back Cairn-Drake bellows a Challenge-Roar before its first swing. Walk into a new zone, or finish a Hearth-Rite Tuck there, and a couplet of its nursery rhyme unfurls above your cross, the coaching words tinted by Sign; a Saffron-Gold border glows round your screen while the Tuck is held.

**The adaptive score.** Every place has its own chamber-folk music, played on real sampled instruments (bassoon, oboe, musette accordion, violin, harp, mandolin, tin whistle, hammered dulcimer, bodhrán): Miller's Oak's bouncy F Lydian waltz, Honey-Wick's 6/8 campfire sway with its tin-whistle tune, Bramble-Bridge's flying waltz (with a little "coming home" cadence as you cross into the square, or step home through a portal), Moss-Oak-Hollow's plucked weave turning into a quirky bassoon-and-oboe duet as you go down Rush-Light-Hollow's sunken lane, Dover-Slap-Falls' march, the wind-swept Ring, and the Cairnsman's gallop. `tools/build_audio_stems.py` composes and records it ([assets/audio/README.md](assets/audio/README.md)); if the recordings are missing, the game plays the same tunes on physically modelled instruments. Four layers play in lock-step and the game turns them up and down: the hush whispers as you near sleeping kin, drums and dulcimer join when a fight starts, the Cairnsman has his own suite (when he's knocked down, a bright harp flourish tells everyone to pile in), and after a fight the music exhales and rests before the tune comes back. Every Sign detonation rings its own chord in the zone's key while the music dips under it, a water-shrine or wind-lantern hushes the dark layer under a harp glissando, hits and arrows sound where they land, and the campfire, the water-shrines, the Chime-Shrine's carillon and the gate's plates have sounds of their own. Chimes, rewards, the victory and the bells are real instruments too. **Miller's Oak and The Crown have Audio Mosaics** ([docs/16 §7](docs/16_ADAPTIVE_AUDIO_ARCHITECTURE.md)) instead of one loop: twelve four-bar phrases each, two in each of six moods (Wonder, Interest, Mellow, Peace, Curiosity, Anxiousness), and the game picks the next phrase on every bar line, so the tune, its chords and the instrument carrying it keep changing and never settle into a loop. Miller's Oak's is a 6/8 mill-wheel tune in F (fiddle, recorder, accordion, violin, oboe and bassoon, cello and low whistle over guitar and hammered dulcimer); The Crown's a 3/4 seaside waltz in G (accordion, violin, oboe, tin whistle over mandolin and a walking pizzicato bass), in place of the five shore suites of its redesign ([docs/23](docs/23_THE_CROWN_ZONE_REDESIGN.md)), which stay baked if you want them back. Towns and campfires lean the phrases toward Peace, Mellow and Wonder, the wild shores, cliffs and galleries toward Curiosity and Anxiousness, high ground toward Wonder; sleeping kin within 35 m turn the next phrase anxious, a fight keeps to the driving phrases, and every minute and a half or so a Peace phrase fades into a few seconds of harp and quiet. Press **F4** to watch it: `Audio: millers_holm [F_LYDIAN 104BPM] · Mood: WONDER (Var B: Violin/Harp) · State: EXPLORE · Stems: … · Voices: …`.

**A storybook world.** Every wall, roof, path and rock is painted in hand-made crafts (limestone flagstones, kiln brick, lime-wash, hewn timber, scalloped shingles, turf, loam and granite), all drawn in code. Bramble-Bridge, Honey-Wick and Oakshire-Wick are stepped-gable houses with half-timbering, jetties, dormers, window boxes and smoking chimneys, round a bakery with a glowing oven, a scribery hung with Sign-coloured vellum and the Oakshire Homestead Hall. Grass, wildflowers, ferns and pebbles grow in every landblock (daisies on the greens, rush-reeds in the hollows, heather on the crags) and sway as you brush past; trees, roofs and arches fade out of the way when they'd hide your hero. Water runs aqua to teal with foam, the canal's wheel splashes, the sky and fog change as you walk from meadow to hollow to highland, and a painted horizon of the atlas's far lands (the cataract mist north, granite tors west, windmills east, chalk cliffs south) rings the view out to 3 km. Each place has its landmarks: Wayfarer-Green's windmill, Pippin-Green's cider mill and furrows, Quarry-Wick's cranes, Bramble-Hollow's willow pools and Nine-Stones-Crossing's ring of standing stones. Chime-Posts at the thresholds ring as you pass and sing that place's rhyme. Round every open Hush-Crack the land greys and the lanterns dim; clear its camp and a golden bloom sweeps out and the crack becomes a mossy cairn ringed with flowers, for good.

**Fellowships and Callings.** Every hero is one of three Fellowships, their raid seat, with one of three Secondary Callings on top; together they name one of nine Callings (06 §3):

| | Stout Heart (+Tank) | Swift Foot (+DPS) | Wise Hand (+Magic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Shield-Sworn** (Tank) | Bridge-Warden | Weald-Captain | Hearth-Guard |
| **The Wayfarers** (DPS) | Iron-Woodsman | Hedge-Runner | Sign-Seeker |
| **The Lantern-Bearers** (Mender) | Bell-Keeper | Wind-Mender | Lantern-Scribe |

A Stout Heart adds 20 health, a wider Perfect Guard and harder poise hits, and unlocks the Iron-Wood Ring-Coat; a Swift Foot adds stamina regen and 15% strike damage, and unlocks the bow and the axe; a Wise Hand earns pips and heals 20% better, holds one more Grimoire Memory, and unlocks the staff. The Secondary Callings open at Level 10 (until then a hero is Unsworn, with their Fellowship's own gear); then each Fellowship starts in its own column.

**The Weapon Channels the Card.** Light and heavy come from your weapon; Forward + heavy, Back + heavy, R and L + R cast the four **Scribe Sign-Cards** in your Grimoire, delivered through that weapon: the Falchion or Broad-Axe swings them with a ribbon trail, the Yew Longbow or Rowan Way-Staff fires them as Sign-bolts and Sign-arrows. Holding L is always Guard, and a guard raised just before a hit is a Perfect Guard (200 ms, 260 ms in heavy plate, +30 ms with a Stout Heart). Armor sets how much damage you shrug off, your stamina, how far you roll and that window.

- **The Shield-Sworn** (Falchion, Iron-Wood Ring-Coat, a banded Greatshield): holds the boss with threat. Shield Charge, Shadow Sanctuary (a shield cone for everyone behind you), Taunt and Bulwark Quake.
- **The Lantern-Bearers** (Rowan Way-Staff, Hearth-Linen Coat, a glowing Tether-Lantern): the D-Pad tethers you to a party member. Sunlit Lance (a Warm-Hearth primer bolt), Green Sanctuary, Homing Heal down the tether, and Lifeline Pull.
- **The Wayfarers** (Yew Longbow, Hedgerow Wool-Mantle, a quiver): the primer specialists. Rising Drive, Frost Crescent and Gale Rush stamp the Signs of Iron-Root, Rime-Gale and Star-Tide; Tempest Flow is the ultimate.
- **The Four Scribe Signs:** a primer card stamps its Sign on an enemy and a teammate's heavy detonates it: Iron-Root into **Shatterpoint** (poise), Rime-Gale into **Glacial Rupture** (damage that splashes nearby foes), Warm-Hearth into **Dawnburst** (party heal) or Star-Tide into **Astral Chorus** (party stamina and pips). Different roles or different Signs make it a x1.5 cross-harmony, and both players earn pips.
- **Card looks:** every invocation blooms its Sign's geometric seal under you and flips an ink card over your shoulder. Cards come in three finishes: **Plain Ink**; **Gilded Leaf**, which adds gold rings and a gold trail; and **Star-Ink**, which raises a starfield dome and leaves glowing footprints.
- **The boss** chases whoever has the most threat, telegraphs a slow Crushing Swipe (its fists rear back and its core glows hotter), and casts raid mechanics: **Crimson Bloom** (red circles under everyone: get out) and **Golden Communion** (a gold circle on one player: stack in to share the damage; half the standing party is enough). At half health it enters **Phase 2** and weaves in the **Doverslap Gale-Beam**: a 3.2 s blue lane aimed at its tank with a "stand behind the tank" shield pillar. Anyone in the tank's Shadow Sanctuary cone takes nothing and earns half a pip; anyone else in the lane takes 40 and is thrown back. Heavy hits and Perfect Guards break its poise for a 5-second knockdown.
- **Victory:** beat it and time slows for 2 seconds, everyone's screen says VICTORY!, the Grimoire levels up, and a reward chest drops in. Walk up to it and brass **Chimes** fly into the party's satchels with a collectible card (Rare and Legendary boss cards, Gilded Leaf and Star-Ink copies); the gold portal then takes everyone home. Sealed Hush-Cracks pay a few Chimes too.

**The Cross-Grimoire HUD.** Since the Phase 12 Kaizen pass the HUD at the bottom of each player's screen is just the four diamonds, with no names or numbers round them: L (guard) on the left, R (your skill) on the right, L+R (your big move) on top and your weapon at the foot, each wearing your own key (Q, E, R on the keyboard; L2, R2, L2+R2 on a pad). Each card diamond shows its Ink Card framed by its Place-Card. A diamond flashes when you press it, sits dimmed until you have the pips for it, and L glows blue while you guard; R rings gold when a teammate's seal is yours to set off. Combo pips (o - o - >) light up under the weapon diamond as you play a form. Health is the red arc on the left, stamina the gold arc on the right (brighter while you sprint), and your Resonance pips are the gems on top. Lantern-Bearers keep their little D-Pad compass of Soul Tethers beside the health arc.

**Jump, sprint and the debug switch.** Space / ✕ jumps (6.5 m/s, about a metre, with a stretch on the way up and a squash when you land), L / ○ rolls (the Quick Dodge), and holding Left Shift / L3 while you move sprints at 1.5x your run, drawing 12 stamina a second; run dry and the sprint stops until you press it again. For playtesting, F2 (or holding Share / View and tapping D-Pad → or ←) steps your hero through Bridge-Warden, Hedge-Runner and Bell-Keeper at Levels 3, 7 and 12, with their gear, Calling and Grimoire, and a "Debug Switch" toast. A switched session is a playtest: nothing is saved after it. Level 12 wears the trail's top gear (Level 10).

**The Seal-and-Tuck Grimoire** ([docs/everwood_equipment_grimoire_architecture.md](docs/everwood_equipment_grimoire_architecture.md)). Your hero keeps one frame for life (a Sword & Board, a Great-Mallet, a Bough-Bow or a Lantern-Staff) and promotes it through seven folkloric materials as the party levels: Orchard-Wood at Lv 1 to 3, Bog-Bronze, Forge-Iron, Rime-Silver, Saffron-Gold, Cairn-Heart, and Star-Glass & Ember-Iron at Lv 20. The tier is paint, not a new model: one shader washes the sculpted weapon in the material's tint and rim and paints a crest on the off hand and a band at the collar. Levels show twice: the True Level the maths run on (1 to 20) and the Renown Rank on the banners (two a level, so a Lv 20 Elder Cairn-Drake is a Rank 40 threat). Before a fight up to three Scribe Cards are pressed onto the weapon, the buckler and the mantle as **seals**: a seal of the Quiet Weir on a buckler takes 35% off every Star-Tide blow and shrugs its stun, the Ember-Bough on a blade adds four to every cut, the Old-Root hits a quarter harder on posture, the West-Wind quickens your stride; the seal's Deep-Ink script glows along the piece in the card's inks, gold-flecked on a Gilded Leaf and star-lit on a Star-Ink. In a fight, land a hit, then press R (or L, and both at the top of the ladder) inside the 18-frame window: the **Pocket-Pull**. In 0.30 s the off hand darts to the pouch, raises a glowing card overhead and slams its light into the weapon; every seal flares, the swing trail takes the card's ink, and for eight seconds your strikes carry the card (1.4x to 1.55x, more with an Under-Card tucked beneath it). The slots open up the ladder: the L card at Lv 3, the buckler seal at Lv 4, R and a second seal from Lv 6, a third seal and the first tuck from Lv 11, the L+R Grand Invocation from Lv 16. The **Seal Ledger** in your cell's bottom-left corner shows the three seal badges, the tier swatch and the L, R and L+R cards with their tucks, by shape and colour, and flips the card you pulled. Heroes stand in Wingfeather youth proportions (a bigger head, hands and boots, and a 1.25x weapon) so a card in a hand reads from the 4.8 m camera. A Squire's one button pulls the card for them when the window is open and the pips are there. Choose your Seals in the Binder's **4. Seal Press** tab at a Hearth-Brazier or the Scribe Desk: up / down picks the weapon, offhand or mantle Seal, left / right presses none, your Fellowship's starting seal or any seal card the party owns (the Reed-Light Scribery sells the four for 40 Chimes each), each row says what that card does on that piece, and your hero wears it at once; the host checks it, every screen sees it, and it is saved for your Fellowship (a playtest saves nothing), while a hero nobody has chosen for wears its role's default hand (the tank's Quiet Weir buckler, the Wayfarers' Ember-Bough blade, the Lantern-Bearers' West-Wind) that grows with the level.

**Options, the map and the Developer Tools (Phase 12.5).** Nothing is written over the top of play any more. Esc (or a tap of Options / Menu on a pad) opens the **Options** menu over that player's view: Audio (four volumes), Video (window, vsync, frame cap, render scale, field of view, World detail, Grass & flowers, Craft textures), Controls (invert Y, camera speed, mouse sensitivity, pad button names, and drawings of the keyboard and the pad with what every key does) and Gameplay (toggle sprint, enemy level badges, the camera pulse, damage numbers, the rhyme ribbon's size, the INK READY border, and each player's help: Master, Adventurer or Squire Gambit for every seat on the screen). Settings save at once to `user://everwood_settings.json`, apart from your progress. With **Toggle Sprint** on (the default) one click of Left Shift / L3 keeps you running until you turn aside, stop or click again. The map (M / R3) is a **Zone Map** of the island you're on, painted from the island itself, with a legend, named towns, dungeons, crossings and quests, and an arrow that turns with you; heavy or Q / E flips to the **World Map** of all five isles. Click a mark or open ground (or choose one and press light) to set a way-marker; fireflies lead you there, by way of the right crossing if it's on another isle. Card tooltips in the Binder say what a card does, when to use it (on the trail, hold its button for 2 s: INK READY; in a fight, tap it in the flash after a hit) and whether your level takes it. Enemies wear a level badge coloured by danger to your party (grey, green, cream, amber, red with a skull): `[Lv. 3]`, `[Lv. 10 Great-Boggart]`, and the boss's on its bar. Everything for testing (the debug hero switch, ending a playtest, the F9 warp, host / join / leave, and the lines the old top banner showed) lives in the **Developer Tools**: F1, or hold Share / View and tap D-Pad ↑, a hazard-striped panel that is never part of the Binder or the Options menu. The Caldera's trees are sculpted oak clumps, ash, weeping willows, orchard apples, tiered pines and layered ferns near the camera, and every elliptical isle's shore wanders into bays, headlands and two to four river inlets (firths on Rime-Firth).

Three assist modes, one per player: **Master** (manual, +15% flow speed), **Adventurer** (bigger input buffer, camera follows the fight, combos chain for you) and **Squire Gambit** (one button picks the move, from range with a bow or staff; you auto-roll out of red circles, your guard goes up by itself when the boss swings at you, and in the Gale-Beam a Squire tank raises Shadow Sanctuary by itself while everyone else rolls out of the lane).

**Fewer than 3 people?** Start the game with `-- --with-companions` and, on the expedition and in the wilds, AI companions (marked "bot") fill whichever of Tank, Healer and DPS nobody is playing. They fight by a KOTOR-style Gambit list (dodge first, then help, then hit), seal Hush-Cracks with you, take a Maker-Seal Plate, get behind the tank's shield for the Gale-Beam (a companion tank raises it), catch up if left behind, and step aside when someone joins. Without it you play alone: a solo hero opens the Gorge gate on one Maker-Seal Plate and meets a 1,400-health Cairnsman.

**Characters** ([docs/26](docs/26_CHARACTER_DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md), the design language). Every person wears a thin warm-umber ink line like a storybook illustration, matte painted clothes (oat linen with soft folds, wool that pills, creased oiled leather), skin with a rosy flush and freckles, and eyes with a bold inked lash line that blink every few seconds. The Level 1 youth comes as **the boy** (a tousled chestnut mop, braces, patched breeches, a carved acorn on a cord) or **the girl** (auburn hair in a braid over her shoulder tied with a ribbon, a laced sage waistcoat, a split riding skirt, a brass button on a cord), chosen on New Game; both wear a hooded heather-blue capelet over their shoulders and have real hands.

**A painted world** ([docs/24](docs/24_PAINTED_WORLD_PASS.md)). Everything is matte and laid on like gouache: three painted tones whose shadow edge wanders along brush strokes, a warm backlight on silhouettes only when the sun is behind them, the sky filling the shadows, and every prop and building weathered (faded and grimy patches, handled edges, moss on its tops). The ground, paths, walls, roofs and timber wear eight hand-painted 1024 px textures. Near you the meadows grow thousands of grass tufts and drifts of daisies, buttercups, poppies, cornflowers and cow parsley; cottages have barrels, crates, woodpiles, sacks and flower tubs about them; the townsfolk are sculpted figures (grown-ups in each Calling's look, and village children) who stand easy and stroll; and the trees' crowns break into clusters at their boughs.

**Storybook heroes.** Every hero is a sculpted storybook figure on a 24-bone skeleton, 4.5 heads tall, built in code: one continuous skinned body (no gaps at the elbows or knees), painted in the Three-Value Harmony so dark boots ground them, the craftwork sits in the middle and the face, brass and Sign veins draw the eye. Each Calling has its own silhouette: the Shield-Sworn's kettle-caps, broad pauldrons and banded Greatshield, the Wayfarers' hoods and hewing pauldrons, the Lantern-Bearers' feather-hats, saffron stoles and a glowing Tether-Lantern at the hip. The **Iron-Woodsman** wears a raised pine-felt hood, a bark-brown beard speckled ash-gray, a heavy Iron-Wood bark pauldron on the left shoulder and a stitched-leather cop on the right, splitting wedges at the belt and a buckler-targe on the back. Faces have real planes: a brow ridge over shadowed eye sockets, a nose, cheekbones, ivory-and-hazel eyes that catch the light, brows, cupped ears under a cap, and a handlebar mustache over the beard, with a warm flush across the nose and cheeks. Wool shows its weave and soft fuzz, timber its grain, leather is matte in its creases and burnished on its worn edges, and forged metal glints in hammered facets. Armour is built in layers that step out over each other: a linen collar and under-sleeves, the coat's split skirt with a rolled leather hem, then the chest (the Wool-Mantle's six stitched-leather brigandine lames with brass rivets and crossed harnesses through roller-buckles, the Ring-Coat's ridged breastplate over ring-mail, the Jerkin's quilted jack laced at the sides, the Linen Coat's pleated surcoat and carillon-clasp), laminated pauldrons, strapped bracers, knuckle plates, folded boot tops and hobnails. Every weapon is sculpted anew at each tier, up to the Doverslap relics' turning Sign-stone. Heroes animate frame by frame with their moves: they run with a jaunty, weight-shifting woodsman's stride (the weapon carried low across the hip, the hips dipping at each step and lofting between), the axe coils and hews, the Earth-Splitter rises and slams, the bow draws to the jaw, the roll tucks into a somersault, the Hearth-Rite kneels, the Truce-Sign bows. The cloak and beard swing with the wind and the run, boots plant on slopes, the off hand rides a two-handed haft, the head turns toward a curious hob or a Glimmer-Spot, and a heavy hit shudders through a hit-stop. Out of a fight for 4 s, the weapon goes on the back. Two players in the same Calling wear different heritage dyes. **The Level 1 youth** ([docs/21](docs/21_WINGFEATHER_YOUTH_HERO.md)): every player hero who is still Unsworn (the trail before Level 10) is drawn as a 14-year-old in homespun, in the spirit of *The Wingfeather Saga* and the Earthsea and Dark-is-Rising children: a wind-tousled chestnut mop, big eyes, a Fellowship-coloured neckerchief, a loose linen shirt with the sleeves rolled, leather braces, patched russet breeches, striped stockings, big turned-down boots and a short heather-blue half-cape with the hood folded down. The youth uses the same 24-bone skeleton, drawn at 0.9 (about 1.6 m, 5.5 heads), with the head sculpted 1.18x and the hands and boots 1.22x. Its own clips: an easy idle (a thumb in a brace, looking about), a walk below 3.2 m/s and an eager, arm-pumping run above it, and a reach-back draw when a fight starts. Swearing a Calling at Level 10 brings the grown hero; companions are always grown. Drop a `.glb` model into `assets/models/` (or `assets/models/heroes/<calling_id>.glb`) to replace any hero, weapon, woodland kin or the boss ([assets/models/README.md](assets/models/README.md)).

## Bramble-Bridge and hot-join

The game starts in the Caldera Isles. The legacy `-- --instance=bramblebridge` starts the old seamless world (`everwood_world`) instead, at the Kindle-Fire just south of **Bramble-Bridge**, and `-- --instance=hub` the classic walled hub (in the files it's still `hub_town`, where the Forest Gate is shut). You come in at the south gate onto the spawn green and walk over the low stone arch the village is named for (no jump needed: Space is the roll) to the village square, with the canal and its watermill turning between them. On the square stand the **Armory & Scribe Desk** and the **Chime-Shrine**, and by its northern Forest Gate the **Raid Portal**: walk into it together (everyone standing on it) for a 3-second countdown into the instanced Doverslap Falls Expedition (`dungeon_dojo`); the gold exit portal there brings you back to whichever Bramble-Bridge you came from. In the seamless world you can instead walk straight out of the gate. The host moves the whole party and the ENet session stays up (`InstanceManager`).

At the **Scribe Desk & Forge**, press light to change your Fellowship, your Calling, your armor and your weapon frame, to **promote** your weapon & offhand or your armour one material tier once your Character Level opens it (your Seals and their Marks come across), and to **draft** a Plain Ink copy of a card the binder lacks for three creature rubbings (up / down picks, left / right changes, dodge closes); you change in place, and a new Fellowship reopens the desk for your new self. Gear your Calling can't use says which Calling unlocks it. Cards live in the Binder and Seals in the Seal Press now (25_GRIMOIRE_GEAR_LEVELING_AND_MENUS_OVERHAUL.md). Each Fellowship's last kit and Calling are saved for its next seat.

At the **Reed-Light Scribery** (the stepped-gable pavilion on the square's west side, its four ink vats glowing in the Sign colours), press light to open its counter: make Ink Cards and chart Place-Cards for 25 Chimes each, or press a card Ready to Press to its next finish (a worn card earns Ink-Binding Marks in fights: 100 for a Gilded Leaf, 250 for Star-Ink). A tap of **Tab / View** anywhere out of combat opens the **Grimoire Binder** on your own screen, in four tabs stepped with Q / E (L / R on a pad): **1. Gear & Weapon** (your hero with Steward Threat as a sum, the kit's totals and the wards its Seals give; the live hero turning in a lit preview with each Seal named where it glows; and the weapon, offhand and mantle as three rows, each with its Seal, finish and Ink-Binding Marks), **2. Quick Magic-Ink Cards**, where it opens: the card bench (the Cross big on the left; your seven-slot hand and Ink-Well; the chosen diamond's three layers, Place-Card, Ink card and the one Tuck; and the Ink Card Collection filtered to that layer, every row saying whether it fits and why not: ← → picks a diamond, ↑ ↓ a layer, light opens the collection and slots a card), **3. Combo Moves** (each form's buttons, and the ones still to learn) and **4. Seal Press** (the three Seals, the cards you can press, what the highlighted card does on each piece and what you'd lose and gain, its Ink-Binding ladder and the preview: ← → picks the piece, ↑ ↓ the card, light presses it, at a Hearth-Brazier or the Scribe Desk; anywhere else it's look-only), plus the Atlas Map. A box under the tabs says, in plain words, what the tab is for and what the highlighted row does.

At the **Chime-Shrine**, press light (J / X / □) to open its menu on your own screen: move up and down, light buys, dodge closes. Upgrades raise the whole party's Max Health or Stamina Regen by 10% a level (5 levels, costing 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20 Chimes). The Chimes, upgrades, card binder, kits, Callings and victories are shared by the party and saved in `user://everwood_progress.cfg` (on a Mac, `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Everwood/`); delete that file to start fresh. Saves from before Chimes keep their old pouch as Chimes. Online, the host's save is the one that counts.

The keyboard and mouse is Player 1. Plugging in a pad joins the next player and splits the screen; unplugging drops them. Tap Enter / Start to join by hand, hold it 1.5 s to leave. Hold Tab / View for 0.6 s to change Fellowship, C to change Calling (on a pad, at the Scribe Desk), M / R3 to change assist mode. Click to steer the camera with the mouse; Esc opens the Options menu and lets the mouse go.

The camera tilts too: the right stick up and down, PageUp / [ and PageDown / ], or the mouse while the right button is held or mouse-look is on (V toggles it). It starts at +22 degrees and goes from -12 (looking up at a crag) to +52 (looking down on a fight); below +18 it drops over your shoulder. A tap of M / R3 out of combat opens the **Wayfarer's Atlas**, the map (the Zone Map, with the World Map a heavy or Q / E away): choose a mark with the stick or arrows, set a way-marker with light, Enter or a click (on a mark or open ground), and a trail of fireflies leads you there. Dodge, M or Esc closes it; inside the Grimoire Binder, M turns to its Atlas Map tab. The minimap in each player's top-right corner shows 160 m round you, the party, the shrines and every Hush-Crack (slate while open, a gold star once calmed). Balance numbers and the reasoning behind them: [docs/04_BALANCE.md](docs/04_BALANCE.md).

## Run it

Open `project.godot` in Godot 4.7 and press F5, or from this folder:

```bash
godot --path .
```

Either way you arrive on Miller's Oak, in the Lee-Shore Orchards. To start at North Quay or on The Crown (a playtest) instead:

```bash
godot --path . -- --boot-zone=north_quay
```

```bash
godot --path . -- --boot-zone=crown
```

To load a legacy map instead (Bramble-Bridge's old seamless world, the classic hub, or Oakshire Vale):

```bash
godot --path . -- --instance=bramblebridge
```

```bash
godot --path . -- --instance=hub
```

Terrain3D is optional and not in git (`addons/` is ignored). Without it the world draws its own ground meshes and plays the same. To draw the ground with Terrain3D on your machine, run this once from this folder, then `godot --headless --path . --import`:

```bash
tools/fetch_terrain3d.sh
```

The game opens on the **title screen** ([docs/22](docs/22_TITLE_SCREEN_AND_TALES.md)): the Everwood wordmark over a living picture of the youth on an orchard knoll above The Sound. **New Game** asks for your hero's name (type one or pick Suggest a Name), starts a fresh Level 1 tale and writes it to its own save file; **Continue** lists every tale on this machine, newest played first (the family's older save shows as "The Family Tale"), and plays the one you pick. ### Playing over the network

One machine presses **F5** to host on port 7777. The others press **F6** to join (it joins `127.0.0.1` unless you start it with `-- --join=192.168.x.x`). Each machine's controllers keep their split-screen seats; the whole party is capped at 5. Players are addressed as `{peer}_{device}`, e.g. `1_0` is the host's keyboard.

Command-line options go after `--`: `--players=N`, `--mode=master|adventurer|squire`, `--host`, `--join=ADDRESS`, `--demo` (players fight on their own), `--weapon=ITEM_ID` and `--armor=ITEM_ID` (this screen's players start with them, e.g. `hunter_bow`, `ironwood_plate`), `--save=PATH` (play from another save file; tests use one so they never touch yours), `--with-companions` (AI companions fill missing roles), `--boot-zone=oakshire|north_quay|crown` (where in the Caldera Isles: the orchards, North Quay, or The Crown's landing as a playtest), `--instance=caldera` (the Caldera Isles, also `oakshire` or `crown`), the legacy `--instance=bramblebridge` (Bramble-Bridge's old seamless world, from the Kindle-Fire), `--instance=hub` (the classic hub) or `--instance=oakshire_vale`, or `--instance=expedition` (the instanced expedition); without it every launch starts in the Caldera Isles (the ids `caldera_isles`, `everwood_world`, `hub_town`, `dungeon_dojo` and `oakshire_vale` work too, and a name it doesn't know is ignored), `--telemetry` (show the F4 badge from the start), `--zone=2` or `--zone=3` (start the expedition at that zone's checkpoint; 3 opens the gate), `--boss-health=0.4` (the Cairnsman's health share when his fight starts, to try Phase 2), `--screenshot=PATH`, `--screenshot-delay=S`, `--quit-after=S` (prints a report: boss health, zone, woodland kin standing, the gate, cards, shots and detonations this peer saw, and each player's weapon).

- Actors build their own components in code. Don't hand-edit the level `.tscn` files or the Landblocks: change `tools/build_levels.gd` (or `tools/build_oakshire_vale.gd` for Oakshire) and run it again. Controls live in project.godot's Input Map, written by `tools/build_input_map.gd`.
- Every tunable number is a named constant at the top of its script. Armor, weapons and Scribe Cards are data in `src/actors/arsenal_catalog.gd` (a new card or weapon is one entry there, or a `.tres` card in `res://data/cards/`); classes and their starter kits are in `src/actors/class_loadouts.gd`, and the nine Callings (names, numbers, gear, trim colour) are registered in `arsenal_catalog.gd` too, read by `src/actors/callings.gd`. Code-built shapes come from `src/actors/shape_kit.gd`.
- Networked combat follows one rule: the machine that owns the target applies the effect. `CombatDirector.deliver()` applies it locally or sends it to the owner, who checks it's plausible first. The host runs the boss, the woodland kin and Oakshire's spirits (and decides parleys and Maker-Seal turns), the gate and the shrine, and tells everyone what happened.
- Knee-high walls, rails and banks sit on their own physics layer (`LowScenery`): bodies bump into them, but cameras see past them.
- Scripts reach the autoloads through each class's `current` (`NetworkManager.current`, `PartyProgress.current`, ...), never by their autoload names (`Network`, `Progress`, ...), so `--check-only` can compile them.

## Check it

```bash
godot --headless --path . --import
```

Then:

```bash
for f in $(find src tools -name '*.gd'); do godot --headless --path . --check-only -s "res://$f"; done
```

```bash
godot --headless --path . --quit
```
