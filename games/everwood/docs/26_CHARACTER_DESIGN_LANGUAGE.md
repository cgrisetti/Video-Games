# 26_CHARACTER_DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md — How Everwood's People Look

The design language for every person in Everwood: the Level 1 youths first
(the boy and the girl), then grown heroes and townsfolk. It sits beside the
world's painted look (docs/24) and the youth's brief (docs/21).

## 1. The one sentence

**Everwood's people are hand-made, warm and alive: sturdy children of field
and hearth, drawn with a storybook painter's ink line, dressed in clothes that
have been worn, mended and loved, with faces that are always about to smile.**

## 2. Where it comes from

| Source | What we take | What we leave |
|---|---|---|
| Miyazaki (Totoro, Kiki, Nausicaä, Howl) | Big, clear eyes with a bold upper lash line and a bright catchlight; open, readable faces; children who are capable, not cute; wind in hair and cloth; simple shapes with a few precise details | Anime gloss, huge heads, tiny noses that vanish |
| The Wingfeather Saga (Shining Isle) | "Truth of Materials": rough-woven linen, oiled leather, wool that pills; puppet-like proportion with weight; families who mend things | Realistic texture noise |
| Fable (the first two) | The cheeky British village: tousled hair, rosy cheeks, a grin with a gap in it; the hero's look telling their story | Grotesque caricature |
| Tolkien | Hobbit practicality: braces, waistcoats, rolled sleeves, sturdy boots or bare feet, pockets for everything | Anything regal |
| Le Guin (Earthsea) | The goatherd's plainness: homespun, a hooded cloak, dark-skinned and light-skinned children alike, dignity without decoration | Wizard costume |
| Susan Cooper (The Dark Is Rising) | Winter-lane children in scarves and wool, an old thing worn as a talisman (a pendant, a ring on a cord) | Gothic darkness |

## 3. Shape

- **Proportion:** about 5 heads to a 1.6 m crown (docs/21). The head is big
  enough to read a face from the 4.8 m camera, never bobble-headed.
- **The silhouette tells the story**, read from far away in three shapes:
  the hair (a tousled mop, a braid), the cape or hood, and the boots. Each
  youth has one asymmetric detail (a patched knee, a braid over one shoulder,
  a sleeve rolled higher on one side).
- **Soft and sturdy, never inflated.** Round where children are round (cheeks,
  calves, the end of the nose), squared where clothes are made (cuffs, boot
  soles, the shoulders of a waistcoat). No balloon limbs, no capsule bodies.
- **One continuous figure.** Clothes overlap the body the way real clothes do:
  sleeves go over the shoulder seam, cuffs over the wrist, the shirt tucks
  under the waistband, a cape drapes over both shoulders. Nothing floats,
  nothing sinks into anything else, in any pose.
- **Hands are hands.** A palm, four fingers with a gentle curl and a thumb,
  drawn a little big (1.22x) so a held card or tool reads. Never a mitten paddle.

## 4. The face

The face carries the joy. Its rules:

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| Eyes | Large, set a little wide and a little low; a **bold, dark upper lash line** that flicks out at the corner; a coloured iris that fills most of the eye, a dark pupil and **one bright catchlight**; a soft lower lid. They **blink** (every 3–6 s, quick) and **glance**. |
| Brows | Thick, soft-ended, tilted **up at the inner end**: hopeful, curious, ready. Never a frown at rest. |
| Nose | Small, rounded, with a **lit tip and a shadowed underside**, a little upturned. Freckles across its bridge. |
| Mouth | A visible, small **closed smile**, corners turned up, a darker line and a hint of lower lip. |
| Cheeks | A painted rosy flush on the cheeks, nose tip and ear tips; **freckles** scattered over the nose and cheeks. |
| Ears | Cupped, shaped (a rim and a hollow), tucked half under the hair. |
| Hair | Big shapes first: a **mass** that covers the crown and frames the face, then a few locks that break the silhouette. Hair has a light side and a shade side, never flat colour. |

## 5. Materials: "Truth of Materials", painted

Every surface is painted the way a background painter paints, in a few
tones with visible strokes, but each material is unmistakable:

| Material | Painted as | Never |
|---|---|---|
| **Linen** (shirts, blouses) | Warm off-white, never pure white; soft fold shadows down from the shoulders and round the elbows; a faint slub weave only up close; a handled grey at the cuffs and collar | A bright, flat white; a fine knit pattern |
| **Wool / canvas** (capes, hoods, waistcoats) | Heavier, deeper colour; a coarse weave; pilling flecks; the hem darker and a little frayed | Smooth, even colour |
| **Leather** (braces, belts, boots, pouches) | Oiled brown, darker along edges and creases, a burnished light where it rubs; **stitching** along straps | Shiny, plastic sheen |
| **Skin** | Warm, with a soft gradient: rosier at cheeks, nose, ears and knuckles; cooler under the jaw; a warm glow where the light turns away (the terminator) | One flat tan |
| **Hair** | Two or three painted tones with a lighter sheen band across the crown | One flat brown |
| **Metal** (buckles, the few there are) | Tin and brass, small, worn | Mirror chrome |

**The ink line.** Every person wears a thin warm-umber ink contour (about two
pixels at any distance) round the silhouette and the big overlaps. This is the
storybook illustration's line, and it is what turns a 3D figure from a toy
into a drawing. It is never black and never thick.

## 6. Colour

- **The 60-30-10 Three-Value Harmony** (docs/19) still holds: grounded darks
  (boots, breeches, cape), crafted mid-tones (leather, linen, the Fellowship
  colour), and a little light (the face, buckles, the Sign).
- **Earthy and joyful:** madder russet, heather blue, moss and Wayfarer green,
  oat linen, oiled tan, chestnut and flax hair. One clear accent per youth (the
  neckerchief, a hair ribbon) in their Fellowship's colour.
- **Skin and hair vary** across the cast: fair and freckled to deep brown;
  chestnut, flax, auburn, black.

## 8. Motion and life

- **Always alive:** breathing in the idle, blinking, glancing about, a weight
  shift; hair and cape answer the wind and the run.
- **Gestures carry character:** the boy's hand on his hip, the girl's
  arms folded or a hand at her braid. Nobody stands like a mannequin.
- **No clipping in any clip**: arm poses keep the forearm in front of, never
  inside, the body.

## 9. Grown-ups and townsfolk

The same language at adult proportion (docs/19): the ink line, the painted
materials, the face rules (brows up at rest, a closed smile, a blink), and the
townsfolk built on the same skeleton (docs/24 §5). Children in the village
are drawn as youths.

## 10. Build notes

**What was built (2026-11-07)**

- **The ink line**: `src/actors/shaders/storybook_ink_outline.gdshader`, the hero
  material's `next_pass` (`HeroMeshAtelier.ink_material()`), warm umber
  `#2B1C14` at 1.7 px on a 1080-line screen; it fades with the hero's own
  near-camera fade. Every hero, youth and townsperson wears it.
- **Painted materials** (`storybook_hero_atelier.gdshader`): the cloth weave cut
  to a faint slub, painted fold strokes and handled patches over it; linen
  warmed to oat and greyed at cuffs and collar; wool pills and darkens at the
  hem; leather creases and mottles; hair in two tones of streaks with a sheen
  band across the crown; skin with freckles (`freckle_amount`) and a rosier
  flush (`malar_mix` from `HeroAppearanceResource.cheek_flush`); lips
  (`HeroMeshAtelier.MAT_LIP`, `lip_color`).
- **Eyes**: a bold inked upper lash line and a blinking lid in the shader
  (`eye_blink`, `eye_center_y`, `eye_half_height`), driven by
  `HeroMeshAtelier.blink_amount()` from ActorVisuals, StorybookFigure and the
  title's diorama: every 3 to 6 s, 140 ms, each person on their own rhythm.
- **The youth's face** (`HeroYouthAtelier`): eyes 1.3x round their centres, set
  a little wide and low; brows lifted at their inner ends; one smooth button
  nose; a visible rose smile and a lower lip; shaped ears (rim, lobe, bowl).
- **Hair**: a fuller cap with a lower hairline (`_hair_cap`), rounder locks.
  The boy's mop (`_hair_mop`); the girl's side fringe, framing locks and braid
  (`_hair_braided`, `_braid`) over her left shoulder and the capelet, tied with
  a Fellowship-coloured ribbon.
- **Joints and seams**: the shirt's shoulders lean onto the clavicle and upper
  arm (`HeroYouthAtelier.shoulder_weights`, also on the braces and capelet); the
  sleeve starts inside the shoulder; the boy's idle is a fist on his hip (the
  thumb-in-brace pose drove his forearm into his chest); the folded hood that
  sat on the shoulders like backpack straps is a draped hooded capelet open at
  the front.
- **Hands**: a palm, four two-jointed fingers in a gentle curl, and a thumb.
- **The girl** (`HeroAppearanceResource.youth_style`, `YOUTH_BOY` / `YOUTH_GIRL`):
  six youth looks in `ArsenalCatalog` (a boy and a girl per Fellowship), her
  laced waistcoat (`_waistcoat`), split riding skirt (`_split_skirt`), brass-button
  talisman (his is a carved acorn: `_talisman`), auburn hair, hazel-brown eyes,
  more freckles, and her own idle (`idle_youth_calm_girl`, a hand at her braid).
- **Choosing**: New Game asks "Who are they, and what are they called?" with
  The Boy and The Girl; the youth on the title's knoll changes as you choose;
  the tale remembers it (`PartyProgress.hero_style`, `[hero] style`). Player 1
  wears the tale's choice; the seats after alternate from it.
- **Checks**: `tools/test_youth_hero.gd` (the ink line, painted face, the girl's
  own sculpt and idle, blinks, shoulder weights, six youth looks) and
  `tools/test_title_screen.gd` (the boy-or-girl choice).

**Deliberate differences**

1. **No expression rig.** Faces don't change expression beyond the blink;
   the rig has no face bones. Brows and mouth are sculpted in their joyful rest.
2. **Grown heroes and townsfolk** get the ink line, blink and materials, but
   not a new face sculpt; that is the next step for them.
3. **The braid hangs from the chest**, not a spring: it doesn't swing yet.
4. **Townsfolk children** draw from all six youth looks.
